Lab 11 · Aim and outcomesOptics

Focal length of a convex lens

Aim

To determine the focal length of a convex lens by the u–v method and hence calculate the power of the lens.

Intended learning outcomes

  • Determine the focal length of a given convex lens by the u–v method.
  • Determine the power of the lens.
  • Represent data graphically and analyse experimental error.

Medical perspectives

The eye's lens and accommodation

The cornea and the crystalline lens form a converging system that focuses images on the retina. The ciliary muscle changes the curvature of the lens, so its focal length and power change: that is accommodation, from a far object to a near one.

Correcting hypermetropia

In long sight the eye focuses behind the retina, so near objects blur. A convex (plus) lens adds converging power. Prescriptions give that power in dioptres, such as +2.50 D, which is a lens of focal length 1 ÷ 2.50 = 0.40 m.

Ophthalmoscopes, magnifiers and microscopes

A convex lens used inside its focal length gives an upright, magnified virtual image, the principle of a magnifier. Ophthalmoscopes, otoscopes and microscopes combine lenses of known power to view the retina, the ear canal and tissue samples.

Lab 11 · TheoryThin convex lens

Where the image forms

A converging lens

A convex lens is thicker at the centre than at the edge. Rays parallel to the principal axis are bent towards it and meet at the principal focus F on the far side. The distance from the optical centre to F is the focal length f.

Light can pass either way, so there is a focus on each side, at the same distance. Twice that distance is marked 2F. A ray through the optical centre passes straight on.

The lens formula

1F = 1U + 1V

u is the distance from the lens to the object and v from the lens to the image. Both are measured from the optical centre, in cm here.

Sign convention used here

Real is positive: distances to real objects and real images are positive, to virtual ones negative. Every reading on the bench is a real object and a real image on the screen, so u, v and f are all positive and 1/F = 1/U + 1/V is used as it stands.

Another common form

With the Cartesian convention (distances measured along the light, object side negative) the same lens obeys 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. Put in u = −30 cm and it gives the same v = +30 cm. The two forms agree; mixing them is what causes sign slips.

Ray diagrams for each case

Two rays from the top of the object are enough: one parallel to the axis, refracted through F; one through the optical centre, undeviated. The image is where they meet.

Why u = 2F gives v = 2F

  1. Put u = 2f into the lens formula.1/v = 1/f − 1/(2f)
  2. The two fractions share the denominator 2f.1/v = 2/(2f) − 1/(2f) = 1/(2f)
  3. So the image is also at 2F, and the magnification is v/u = 1: same size, inverted.v = 2f, m = 1

With f = 15 cm: u = 30 cm gives v = 30 cm. This is the reading that sits in the middle row of the table, between the diminished and magnified groups.

The shortest object-to-image distance: u + v = 4F

Write u = f + x and v = f + y, where x and y are the distances beyond each focus. The lens formula becomes x y = f² (Newton's form).

Then u + v = 2f + x + y, and for a fixed product x y = f² the sum x + y is smallest when x = y = f. So u + v ≥ 4f, with equality only at u = v = 2f.

On the bench: the screen can never catch a real image closer than 4F from the object. For a 15 cm lens, lamp and screen must be at least 60 cm apart.

Why the graph of 1/v against 1/u is a straight line

Rearranged, the lens formula reads 1/v = −1/u + 1/f: a straight line of slope −1. Where it meets the 1/v axis (1/u = 0), 1/v = 1/f; where it meets the 1/u axis (1/v = 0), 1/u = 1/f. Both intercepts equal 1/F, so their mean is the best value, and F = 1 ÷ mean intercept. Drawing one best-fit line through all the points averages out the scatter of the single readings.

Power of a lens

The power is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres: P = 1/f. Its unit is the dioptre, D = m⁻¹. A converging lens has positive power, a diverging lens negative.

For f = 15 cm = 0.15 m, P = 1 ÷ 0.15 ≈ +6.67 D. A shorter focal length bends light more strongly and has a larger power. Thin lenses in contact add their powers: P = P₁ + P₂, which is why prescriptions are written in dioptres.

Units

f must be in metres. 15 cm used directly gives 1 ÷ 15 = 0.0667, a hundred times too small. 15 cm = 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15 m.

Sources of error

Parallax

Reading the index from an angle shifts it against the scale. The eye goes square to the scale, over the hairline of the rider window.

Range of sharpness

The image looks sharp over a short range of screen positions (depth of focus), longer for magnified images. Setting the screen in the middle of that range halves the error.

Index error

The rider index may not lie exactly under the optical centre or the object plane. A known offset is added to every reading, or measured with a rule.

Alignment

The object, the lens centre and the screen centre must be on one horizontal axis, at the same height, with the lens face square to the bench.

Distance object

A window 10 m away is not at infinity: its image forms at 1000 × 15 ÷ 985 ≈ 15.2 cm, a little beyond F. That is why it gives only a rough F.

Thin-lens model

A real lens has thickness, so u and v strictly run from its principal planes. For a thin lens the optical centre is close enough.

Good practice
  • Read each rider index with the eye square to the scale, over the hairline, to avoid parallax.
  • Find the range over which the image looks sharp by moving the screen both ways, then set it in the middle.
  • Keep the object, the centre of the lens and the centre of the screen at the same height on one axis.
  • Use a dimmed room so the image on the screen is easy to judge.
  • Space the readings: three well beyond 2F, one at 2F, three between F and 2F.
Care
  • The lamp house gets hot: move it by its rider, not by the case.
  • Hold the lens by its holder and keep fingerprints off the glass.

Common slips

u and v swapped

u runs from the lens to the object (the lamp), v from the lens to the screen. Swapping them puts each row in the wrong group.

Forgetting the reciprocal

1/F = 0.0667 cm⁻¹ is not the focal length. F = 1 ÷ 0.0667 ≈ 15.0 cm.

Power with f in cm

1 ÷ 15 = 0.0667 is a hundred times too small. f = 0.15 m, so P = 1 ÷ 0.15 = 6.67 D.

One intercept only

Both intercepts estimate 1/F. Their mean uses the whole line and cancels part of the scatter.

Sum instead of mean

The experimental F is the mean of the seven F values, not their sum; the mean intercept is (a + b) ÷ 2.

Sign slips

Real-is-positive: on the bench u, v, f and the intercepts are all positive, and a convex lens has a positive power.
